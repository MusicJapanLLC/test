// Idioms & expressions people actually use
a piece of cake | phr | 朝飯前 | very easy | The test was a piece of cake. || Latte art looked hard, but it's a piece of cake now. || Setting up the mic? Piece of cake. | study | 会話では a を省いて Piece of cake. とも言う。easy peasy はさらに子どもっぽい。
break a leg | phr | 頑張って（舞台前） | good luck before a show | Break a leg tonight! || Break a leg at your open mic! || You got your first gig? Break a leg. | music | ライブや舞台前の「頑張って」。試験や面接の前にはあまり使わない。
hit the books | phr | 勉強する | study hard | I need to hit the books this weekend. || I have a test Monday, so I'm hitting the books. || Time to hit the books. That English exam won't pass itself. | study | 試験前に集中して勉強するイメージ。study hard よりカジュアル。
hit the sack | phr | 寝る | go to bed | I'm gonna hit the sack. || I hit the sack right after my night shift. || It's 2 a.m. Time to hit the sack. | daily | hit the hay も同じ意味。go to bed のくだけた言い方。
call it a day | phr | 今日は終わりにする | stop working for today | Let's call it a day. || We've done ten takes. Let's call it a day. || I'm calling it a day. My voice is gone. | work | 夜なら call it a night とも言う。仕事や練習を切り上げるときの定番。
under the weather | phr | 体調が悪い | a little sick | I'm feeling under the weather. || I'm a little under the weather, so I'll skip the gym. || Sorry, my voice is rough. I'm under the weather. | health | 軽い体調不良の婉曲表現。重い病気には使わない。
on the same page | phr | 認識が一致して | agreeing, understanding the same way | Are we on the same page? || Let's get on the same page before the client call. || The band isn't on the same page about the setlist. | work | get on the same page で「認識をそろえる」。仕事でもバンドでも使える。
the last straw | phr | 我慢の限界 | the final problem that makes you quit | Being late again was the last straw. || He ate my lunch again. That's the last straw. || The rent increase was the last straw, so we moved. | conflict | That's the last straw! で「もう我慢の限界」の怒りを表す。
a blessing in disguise | phr | 不幸中の幸い | something bad that turns out good | Losing that job was a blessing in disguise. || Missing that flight was a blessing in disguise. I met my girlfriend at the airport. || The rain was a blessing in disguise. The cafe was packed. | feelings | 「災い転じて福となす」に近い。後から振り返って言うことが多い。
cost an arm and a leg | phr | すごく高い | be very expensive | That watch cost an arm and a leg. || Studio time costs an arm and a leg in Tokyo. || Fixing my laptop cost an arm and a leg. | money | 主語は値段のかかる物。very expensive の大げさでくだけた言い方。
once in a blue moon | phr | めったにない | very rarely | I go to the movies once in a blue moon. || My roommate cleans once in a blue moon. || I drink, but only once in a blue moon. | daily | rarely のくだけた言い方。文末に置くのが自然。
the elephant in the room | phr | 誰も触れない大問題 | an obvious problem no one mentions | Let's talk about the elephant in the room: the budget. || Nobody mentioned the elephant in the room. He was two hours late. || Okay, elephant in the room. Are we breaking up the band? | business | みんな気づいているのに触れない問題。address the elephant in the room（あえて触れる）が定番。
think outside the box | phr | 型にはまらず考える | think creatively | We need to think outside the box. || You don't need a big budget. Just think outside the box. || Let's think outside the box and sell coffee at the show. | business | ビジネスで使われすぎて陳腐に聞こえることもあるので多用は避ける。
cut corners | phr | 手を抜く | do something cheaply or badly | Don't cut corners on quality. || We never cut corners on our coffee beans. || If you cut corners on the warm-up, you'll get hurt. | work | お金や時間を節約するために「手を抜く」。悪い意味で使う。
get the hang of | phr | コツをつかむ | learn how to do | You'll get the hang of it. || I'm finally getting the hang of the espresso machine. || It took me a month to get the hang of night shifts. | study | get the hang of it の形が圧倒的に多い。get used to（慣れる）と近い。
keep an eye on | phr | 見張る・注意して見る | watch carefully | Can you keep an eye on my bag? || Keep an eye on the oven. The muffins burn fast. || Keep an eye on that guy. He's been here three hours. | daily | 「ちょっと見ててくれる？」の定番。watch より軽く気軽。
let the cat out of the bag | phr | 秘密をばらす | reveal a secret | Who let the cat out of the bag? || I accidentally let the cat out of the bag about the surprise party. || Don't let the cat out of the bag. The single isn't out yet. | friends | うっかり秘密をもらすこと。spill the beans もほぼ同じ意味。
miss the boat | phr | 機会を逃す | lose a chance | Book now or you'll miss the boat. || I missed the boat on cheap rent in this area. || Everyone's already on that app. Did we miss the boat? | business | チャンスや流行に乗り遅れること。miss out も近い。
on the fence | phr | 迷っている | undecided | I'm on the fence about moving. || I'm still on the fence about quitting the cafe. || She's on the fence about a second date. | feelings | on the fence about ~ の形。決めかねて中立にいるイメージ。
out of the blue | phr | 突然 | unexpectedly | She texted me out of the blue. || My old producer called out of the blue. || Out of the blue, the owner gave everyone a bonus. | crush | 予想外の連絡や出来事に使う。suddenly より「思いがけず」の感じが強い。
play it by ear | phr | 臨機応変にやる | decide as you go | Let's play it by ear. || I don't have a setlist. I'll play it by ear. || Dinner after church? Let's play it by ear. | friends | もとは楽譜なしで耳で演奏すること。予定を決めずに様子を見る意味。
pull someone's leg | phr | からかう | joke with someone | Relax, I'm pulling your leg. || Are you pulling my leg, or did you really quit? || He's pulling your leg. The cafe isn't closing. | friends | 進行形で使うことが多い。You're pulling my leg! で「冗談でしょ」。
see eye to eye | phr | 意見が一致する | agree | We don't always see eye to eye. || My producer and I don't see eye to eye on the drums. || We finally see eye to eye on the budget. | conflict | 否定文で使うことが多い。see eye to eye on ~（〜で意見が合う）。
speak of the devil | phr | 噂をすれば | the person we're talking about just arrived | Speak of the devil! We were just talking about you. || Speak of the devil. Your ex just walked in. || Speak of the devil, here comes my boss. | friends | 日本語の「噂をすれば」にぴったり。悪い意味はない冗談の言い方。
take it easy | phr | 気楽にね | relax | Take it easy this weekend. || Take it easy on the new guy. It's his first day. || Take it easy. It's just a rehearsal. | feelings | take it easy on ~ で「〜に手加減して」。別れ際の挨拶にも使う。
the ball is in your court | phr | 次はあなたの番 | it's your decision now | I sent the offer. The ball's in your court. || I said sorry. The ball is in her court now. || We made our offer. The ball is in their court. | business | テニスが語源。相手に判断を委ねたときに使い、your を her / their に変えてもOK。
time flies | phr | 時が経つのは早い | time passes quickly | Time flies when you're having fun. || Time flies. I've worked here five years. || Wow, it's already midnight? Time flies. | daily | 単独で Time flies. と言ってもOK。時間の早さに驚いたときの定番。
under pressure | phr | プレッシャーの中で | stressed by demands | I work well under pressure. || I make my best beats under pressure. || Don't put me under pressure. I'll mess up. | work | put someone under pressure（プレッシャーをかける）。面接の定番フレーズでもある。
up in the air | phr | 未定 | not decided | Our plans are still up in the air. || The tour dates are still up in the air. || Everything's up in the air until the visa comes through. | daily | まだ決まっていない状態。undecided のくだけた言い方。
when pigs fly | phr | ありえない | never | He'll be on time when pigs fly. || My roommate will clean the bathroom when pigs fly. || Me, waking up at five? When pigs fly. | friends | 「ありえない」の皮肉。単独で When pigs fly. と返すこともできる。
a no-brainer | phr | 考えるまでもない | an easy decision | Free upgrade? No-brainer. || Taking the higher-paying shift was a no-brainer. || Free coffee for staff? That's a no-brainer. | daily | 迷う必要がない簡単な決断。no-brainer だけでも通じる。
back to square one | phr | 振り出しに戻る | start again | The client said no. Back to square one. || The file got deleted, so we're back to square one. || We broke up, and now I'm back to square one. | business | すごろくの「振り出し」のイメージ。努力が無駄になった悔しさを含む。
behind someone's back | phr | 陰で | secretly | Don't talk about him behind his back. || She was dating someone else behind my back. || They changed the schedule behind my back. | conflict | 陰口や裏切りに使う。反対は to someone's face（面と向かって）。

mumble | v | ぼそぼそ話す | speak unclearly | Stop mumbling. I can't hear you. || I mumble when I'm nervous. || He mumbled something and left. | study | 聞き取りにくい話し方全般。不満のつぶやきは mutter。
stutter | v | どもる | repeat sounds while speaking | I stutter when I'm nervous. || My laptop stutters when I open too many tracks. || He stuttered through his whole speech. | feelings | 機械や映像が「カクつく」意味でも使う。
chat | v | おしゃべりする | talk casually | We chatted for an hour. || Let's chat after the service. || I chatted with a regular about jazz all morning. | friends | 名詞で have a chat。オンラインの「チャット」も同じ語。
mutter | v | つぶやく | speak quietly, often complaining | He muttered something about the boss. || She muttered something and walked off. || Stop muttering. If you're mad, just say it. | work | 不満や悪口を小声でつぶやくイメージ。mutter under your breath が定番。
tease | v | からかう | make fun of playfully | Stop teasing me! || My coworkers tease me about my accent. || I'm just teasing. Your song is great. | friends | 愛情のあるからかい。悪質ないじめは bully。
compliment | v | 褒める | say something nice | He complimented my English. || A customer complimented my latte art today. || Thanks for the compliment! | friends | complement（補う）とつづりが似ているので注意。名詞でも動詞でも使う。
insult | v | 侮辱する | say something rude | Don't insult the chef. || Is that a compliment or an insult? || He insulted my music, so I left. | conflict | 名詞は前、動詞は後ろにアクセントが来る。
reassure | v | 安心させる | make less worried | The doctor reassured me. || I tried to reassure the new guy that he's doing fine. || It's reassuring to know you're there. | health | 形容詞 reassuring（ほっとさせる）もよく使う。assure は「保証する」でやや硬い。
comfort | v | 慰める | make someone feel better | She comforted me when I was sad. || I tried to comfort her, but I said the wrong thing. || Ramen is my comfort food after a long shift. | feelings | comfort food（ほっとする料理）、comfort zone も頻出。
cheer | v | 応援する・歓声を上げる | shout to support | The crowd cheered. || Everyone cheered when the cake came out. || Cheer up. It's just one bad show. | music | cheer up（元気出して）、cheer for ~（応援する）。乾杯は Cheers!
hug | v | ハグする | hold someone in your arms | Come here, let me hug you. || Can I get a hug? || We hugged it out after the fight. | friends | 名詞でも使う（give someone a hug）。hug it out は仲直りのハグ。
cry | v | 泣く | shed tears | That movie made me cry. || I cried at my friend's wedding. || It's okay to cry. | feelings | 静かに涙ぐむのは tear up。cry は声を出して泣くイメージ。
laugh | v | 笑う | make sounds when something's funny | I couldn't stop laughing. || Don't laugh. I'm serious. || We laughed so hard the manager came over. | friends | laugh at ~ は「〜をばかにして笑う」の意味にもなるので注意。
smile | v | 微笑む | make a happy face | Smile! It's a photo. || She smiled at me from across the room. || Customers smile more when you smile first. | daily | smile at ~（〜に微笑む）。ニヤリは grin、作り笑いは fake smile。

procrastination | n | 先延ばし癖 | delaying things | Procrastination is my superpower. || My procrastination is getting out of hand. || Cleaning my room is just procrastination with extra steps. | work | 動詞は procrastinate。勉強や仕事の先延ばしの話題で頻出。
motivation | n | やる気 | the reason or desire to do | I have zero motivation today. || Watching live shows gives me motivation. || What's your motivation for learning English? | feelings | 形容詞は motivated。「モチベが下がる」は lose motivation。

faith | n | 信仰・信頼 | belief, trust | Have a little faith. || My faith got me through a hard year. || I have faith in this band. | faith | have faith in ~（〜を信じる）。宗教だけでなく人や計画への信頼にも使う。
pray | v | 祈る | talk to God | I pray before big decisions. || We pray together before every show. || I'm praying it doesn't rain on the festival. | faith | pray for ~（〜のために祈る）。冗談で「頼むから〜であってくれ」の意味でも使う。
